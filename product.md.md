# Portfolio Website

## Overview

This website is my primary professional portfolio as a Product Designer. It showcases selected work, my design thinking, process, craft, and impact through detailed case studies. The portfolio should communicate professionalism, attention to detail, and a high standard of execution while remaining approachable and easy to navigate.

The website should feel like a well-designed product rather than a marketing site. Every interaction, transition, and layout decision should reinforce the quality of my work without distracting from the content.

---

# Goals

The website should:

- Help recruiters and hiring managers quickly understand who I am and what I design.
- Showcase my strongest UX and product design case studies.
- Demonstrate my design process, decision making, and problem-solving skills.
- Reflect my attention to detail through thoughtful interaction design and visual polish.
- Make it easy for visitors to contact me or view my resume.
- Scale easily as I add future projects.

---

- What kind of designer am I?
- What industries have I worked in?

- Why should they continue exploring?

# Password Protected Projects

Some case studies contain confidential work.

Requirements:

- Clean password entry page
- Session remains unlocked until browser session ends
- Protected projects are clearly indicated before opening
- Public projects should remain directly accessible
- Password protection should be easy to configure for future projects

---

# Content Principles

The work should always be the focus.

Avoid unnecessary marketing copy.

Prioritize:

- Clear storytelling
- Strong visuals
- Honest reflections
- Readability
- Breathing room

The portfolio should feel authentic rather than overly polished or self-promotional.

---

# UX Principles

Navigation should be effortless.

Users should never wonder:

- Where am I?
- What should I click next?
- How do I return?

Scrolling should feel natural.

Images should load gracefully.

Transitions should support orientation rather than become visual distractions.

---

# Performance

The website should feel fast.

Requirements:

- Optimized images
- Lazy loading where appropriate
- Responsive images
- Excellent Lighthouse scores
- Minimal layout shift
- Fast page transitions

---

# Accessibility

The portfolio should meet WCAG AA standards.

Requirements:

- Keyboard navigation
- Visible focus states
- Semantic HTML
- Screen reader friendly
- Accessible color contrast
- Reduced motion support

---

# SEO

Each project should have:

- Unique metadata
- Open Graph image
- Descriptive URLs
- Structured headings

The website should be indexable except for password-protected projects.

---

# Future Expansion

The website should be easy to extend.

Potential future additions:

- More case studies
- Writing
- Side projects
- Design experiments
- Photography
- Speaking
- Resume updates

The architecture should support growth without requiring a redesign.

# What Success Looks Like

When someone finishes browsing the portfolio, they should feel that:

- I solve meaningful product problems.
- I care deeply about craft and execution.
- I can think strategically as well as visually.
- I communicate clearly.
- I would be enjoyable to work with.

The portfolio itself should demonstrate the same level of care and quality that I bring to designing products.

# Implementation Principles

- Prefer reusable components over one-off implementations.
- Follow a mobile-first responsive approach.
- Maintain consistent spacing, typography, and interaction patterns throughout the site.
- Avoid introducing new UI patterns unless they solve a clear user problem.
- Keep the codebase modular and easy to extend with additional case studies and pages.
- Favor clean, maintainable code over clever abstractions.
- Use animations sparingly to improve orientation and perceived quality, never as decoration.
- Treat every page as production-ready, with proper loading, error, and empty states where applicable.
